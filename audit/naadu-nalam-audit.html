1. Summary

Verdict: not ready to launch. The look is consistent: one font family, two brand colours, clean product pages, and Lighthouse scores 100 for accessibility, SEO and best practices. Three things block a launch, though. The checkout never sends an order. The home page has a 2,400px scroll-scrubbed animation that leaves the menu landing on empty arches. And the footer fills the whole screen on every page. On top of that there are visible problems with the product photos (an AI-generator watermark, misspelled text on a sack, one photo reused for several products) and a lot of loose spacing.

2. Top issues

C1CriticalNo design change

"Place order" doesn't send the order anywhere

/checkout · all viewports · src/lib/order.ts:74
What
submitOrder() waits 900ms, invents an order number (NN + the last 6 digits of the timestamp), shows the confirmation and empties the cart. Nothing reaches the store, email or the WooCommerce backend the catalogue came from.
Why
A real customer would see "order placed" and the shop would never hear about it.
Fix
Post to the WooCommerce Store API (or a route handler that emails the order) before showing the confirmation, and show an error if that fails. Until then, don't put the site live with checkout enabled.
C2CriticalChanges design (motion only)

Panchabhootham + 7 Generations is one pinned, scroll-scrubbed stage of about 2,400px

/ · 1440×900 and 1280×720 · HeritageJourney.tsx (cine:h-[360svh] + cine:sticky)
What
On wide screens the section is 360% of the screen height with a sticky stage inside. For 2,369px of scrolling at 1440 (1,914px at 1280) the page doesn't move: the scroll only drives the animation. Clicking Panchabhootham in the menu lands on five empty sand-coloured arches, because the images only fill in as you keep scrolling. Clicking 7 Generations lands mid-animation with just one generation label drawn. The QA script measured this as 2,369px of empty space and a "7 Generations" landing 1,709px off.
Why
Visitors feel stuck, the menu shows half-finished sections, and it breaks the rule of no pinned or stacked sections.
Fix
Make two ordinary sections, each one screen tall, with all five elements and all seven labels visible at rest (at most a short fade-in once). Keep the arch artwork, the cards and the layout exactly as they are.
C3CriticalChanges design

The footer fills the whole screen, so the last section never shows with it

every page · 1440×900, 1280×720, 390×844 · Footer.tsx (min-h-[calc(100svh-77px)])
What
The footer is a full-height block: a "FROM OUR LAND TO YOUR FAMILY" CTA band, a headline, a button, then the link columns and a photo. It's 813px tall at 1440×900 and takes the entire screen under the header at 1280×720. The script's footer-fit check failed on all 10 desktop runs. Inside the footer there are also 118px of empty space above and below the content (95px on mobile).
Fix
A short footer, at most 40% of the screen: logo with one line of text, the link columns, the social icons, and the copyright row. Move the "Seven generations of wellbeing" CTA into the last home section, or drop it. Header menu and footer links should both be 16px.

Product photos: AI watermark, misspelled sack text, reused photos, label artwork mixed in

/shop, product pages, quick view, recommendations · all viewports · src/lib/catalog.ts, public/images/shop/products

Remove the sparkle (crop or inpaint from the originals, then re-run npm run images). Replace the Barley photo. Ask the client for real photos of the 8 shared and 17 label-only products; until then, show the label artwork centred on the same cream card background so it reads as a deliberate packshot.

With images.unoptimized: true (correct for Vercel quota), Next.js serves exactly one file per image and ignores sizes, so there's no srcset. 4. Spacing over 5px

Your rule is that no spacing should be more than 5px. I applied it to the space between sections, the empty space between a section's edge and its content, and the gaps between cards. Spacing inside a component (between a heading and its text, button padding) isn't counted, because bringing that to 5px would make things congested, which is the other thing you asked to avoid. 6. Image configuration plan (for the implementation round, not applied)

The audit changed no configuration. For the fix round:

  1. Keep the existing images block and its unoptimized: true; merge any new keys into it rather than replacing it. Nothing else in next.config.ts needs to change.
  2. Pre-generate widths locally in scripts/optimize-images.mjs: e.g. hero-family-home-828.avif / -1440 / -1983, cat-*-500 / -1000, elements at 640×800. AVIF first, with WebP only where AVIF isn't smaller.
  3. Serve them responsively without the Vercel optimizer: a plain <picture> / <img srcSet sizes>, or getImageProps() from next/image with a manual srcSet. Either way the browser picks the right width and no /_next/image requests are made.
  4. Loading: hero preload / fetchPriority="high" (already set); everything below the first screen loading="lazy" (already the default); always pass width/height or fill + sizes to avoid layout shift.
  5. Compression targets: photos AVIF q55–62 / WebP q72–78; cut-outs with alpha WebP q80, alpha q90; nothing over 150KB except the hero at desktop width.
// next.config.ts: unchanged apart from what's already there

  unoptimized: true,               // no Vercel Image Optimization quota
  formats: ["image/avif", "image/webp"],   // inert while unoptimized
  qualities: [75, 85],

7. What passed

  • No horizontal scroll, no section overlap, nothing covering another section, and no gaps between sections, at any size.
  • No video controls or play/pause buttons, and no slide numbers.
  • No links back to the original site (naadunalam.fastfilings.in).
  • One font family (Albert Sans, with Noto Sans Tamil for Tamil script) and two brand colours (forest green, gold).
  • No broken images, no blurry images by the script's measure, no console errors, no cursor effects.
  • Lighthouse mobile: accessibility 100, best practices 100, SEO 100. Real title and meta description, lang="en", skip link, every icon button labelled.
  • Checkout form has visible labels, required markers and an error summary.

8. What wasn't checked

  • Placing a real order: there's no backend to receive one (C1).
  • All 82 product pages: one page (Barley Rice) and the quick view were reviewed by hand. All 138 product image files were checked as files (duplicates, sizes); the watermark was confirmed by eye on 5 products, not on every one.
  • Lighthouse Performance score: not run. Measured transfer sizes and image dimensions instead.
  • Safari, Firefox and real phones: Chromium only. Touch scrolling on the Panchabhootham carousel was not tested on a device.
  • The live Vercel deployment: only the local production build. The live site may be on an older commit (the font change isn't committed).
  • Tamil language mode: there isn't one (H4).
  • Keyboard-only walkthrough: Lighthouse checks passed, but tabbing through the mega menu, quick view and filters wasn't done by hand.
